Shopify vs WooCommerce for Indian brands — which is right for you?

By Saravanan M. Published: 15 June 2026 11 min read

We've built and migrated stores on both. Neither is objectively better — but one is almost always better for you specifically. Here's the honest India-focused breakdown we use with our own clients.

90-second answer Pick Shopify if you value speed, low ops overhead, and predictable monthly costs. Pick WooCommerce if you need heavy customisation, own the code long-term, or run a store with unusual workflows. For most first-time Indian D2C brands under ₹1Cr GMV, Shopify wins.

The 30-second comparison

Shopify

// Hosted SaaS · Founded 2006
  • Zero server management
  • Monthly subscription (₹2K+)
  • App-store extensibility
  • Fixed template flexibility
  • No lock-in-free code ownership
  • Transaction fee if you skip Shopify Payments

WooCommerce

// Open-source WordPress plugin · Founded 2011
  • Full code ownership
  • No monthly platform fee
  • Huge plugin ecosystem
  • You manage hosting + security
  • Total control of design/features
  • You own maintenance risk

The India-specific factors that actually matter

Most "Shopify vs WooCommerce" articles online are US-centric. Here's what changes when you're selling in India.

1. Payment gateways

Both platforms support all major Indian gateways — Razorpay, Cashfree, PayU, CCAvenue, Instamojo. Setup takes 30 minutes on either. UPI, cards, wallets, and pay-later options work on both.

The one nuance: on Shopify, if you don't use Shopify Payments (which has limited India coverage), you pay an extra 2% "platform transaction fee" on top of the gateway's 2%. That's 4% total on every domestic order. On Shopify Advanced (₹32K/month), this drops to 0.5%. On WooCommerce, you only pay the gateway's 2%.

2. GST invoicing

Both platforms support GST-compliant invoices — but via third-party apps/plugins, not natively. Shopify needs the WebPlanex GST Invoice app (~₹800/month). WooCommerce needs the Woo India GST plugin or a manual template edit. WooCommerce is slightly more flexible if your CA wants specific HSN code handling.

3. Shipping & logistics integrations

Shopify has native Shiprocket, Delhivery, and Bluedart integrations via the app store. WooCommerce has Shiprocket and Nimbuspost plugins. Both handle multi-warehouse dispatch, COD, and reverse logistics. No meaningful difference.

4. Hosting & latency for Indian users

Shopify runs on their global CDN — pages load fast for Indian users automatically. No decision needed.

WooCommerce depends on your hosting choice. Shared hosting (₹300/month) is a false economy — stores time out at peak traffic. Use managed WordPress hosting like Cloudways with an AWS Mumbai instance (~₹1,500/month) or Kinsta (~₹2,500/month) for real e-commerce performance.

5. Content marketing (blog)

Both have blog engines. WooCommerce runs on WordPress, so its blogging is more mature — better SEO plugins (Yoast, Rank Math), more editorial control. Shopify's blog is functional but limited.

If content marketing is a core channel for you, WooCommerce has a slight edge.

6. Custom checkout flows

If you have a regulated category, a subscription model, or a custom quotation flow, WooCommerce wins hands-down. You can rewrite checkout logic in PHP. Shopify's checkout is only customisable on Shopify Plus (~₹1.5L/month).

Break-even math

Below ~₹1Cr GMV, cost is a wash. Between ₹1–5Cr, WooCommerce starts to look cheaper (~₹1.5L/year savings). Above ₹5Cr, Shopify Plus starts making sense again for its infrastructure and support quality.

Where founders go wrong They pick WooCommerce to save the ₹2K/month subscription, then spend ₹2L on getting the site to a state Shopify would've delivered in a week. If your time costs more than ₹500/hour, Shopify usually wins on TCO — you're really paying for saved dev hours.

Where each one is clearly better

Pick Shopify if…

  • You're launching your first store and want it live in under 30 days
  • You don't have a technical co-founder or in-house developer
  • You're expecting variable/spiky traffic (festive sales, ad campaigns)
  • You want predictable monthly costs, not surprise dev bills
  • You prioritise reliability and mobile checkout speed over customisation
  • You're planning to scale internationally later

Pick WooCommerce if…

  • You have (or hire) a WordPress developer on retainer
  • Content marketing / blog is a primary channel for you
  • You need custom checkout flows (subscriptions, quotations, B2B pricing tiers, regulated categories)
  • You need deep third-party integrations that Shopify doesn't have
  • You have strict data sovereignty needs and want to host in India
  • Your GMV projections suggest ₹2Cr+ within 18 months

Our verdict for Indian brands, 2026

If you're in the "figuring it out" stage (first store, first 12 months, under ₹1Cr GMV) — go Shopify. Ship fast, learn what customers want, spend your time on marketing not on WordPress security patches.

If you're past that (established brand, clear roadmap, ₹1Cr+ GMV, or specific technical needs) — go WooCommerce. The cost/control ratio flips in your favour.

The worst outcome is buying the platform that doesn't match where you actually are. Migrations cost ₹40K–₹1L and 6+ weeks of downtime risk. Choose deliberately.
